# Story Prompt Template

**Input:** Name of a person and/or brand: **{NAME}**

# **Instructions**

1. **Research {NAME}** (and the business they founded, if applicable). Collect key facts:  
   - early dream(s)  
   - quirks, unusual talents, abilities that make them different than their peers
   - their **scrappiest beginning** (financial hardship, personal struggle, very low point, unusual childhood)
   - moments they were ridiculed, rejected, ignored, or doubted  
   - key setbacks/failures/handicaps
   - creative, bold, unexpected, out-of-the-box solutions
   - shocking or game-changing wins
   - current scale of success (net worth, revenue, valuation, reach)

2. **Transform the research into a cinematic narrative**
   - Don’t summarize; dramatize.  
   - Write like you’re narrating a movie trailer — emotional, tense, and vivid.  
   - Each page should feel like a *scene*, not a paragraph.  
   - Use **sensory detail** — what they saw, heard, felt, feared, hoped.
   - Use **a gritty, human voice** — admire their drive but expose their flaws, doubt, and persistence.  
   - Use facts to anchor the story, but let *emotion* drive it.

3. **Narrative pacing (Page structure)**
   - **Page 0 (Hook):**  
       - 1 sentence only.  
       - Cinematic tagline connecting a scrappy beginning to a numeric success metric today.  
       - Example: “From broke kid to $150M Oscar winner.”  

   - **Page 1 (The Small World):**  
       - Show the early environment — constraint, limitation, struggle.  
       - Focus on sensory detail: heat, noise, smell, silence, isolation.  
       - 3 sentences, short and sharp.  

   - **Page 2 (The Leap):**  
       - The first major risk or leap of faith — financial, emotional, or physical.  
       - 4 sentences; mix of tension and hope.  

   - **Page 3 (The Wall):**  
       - Moment of rejection, ridicule, or failure.  
       - Keep the rhythm tense — short sentences, clipped thoughts.  
       - 3 sentences max.  

   - **Page 4 (The Defiance):**  
       - The creative or stubborn act that breaks the pattern.  
       - The rebellion that changes their trajectory.  
       - 4 sentences, one long emotional line allowed.  

   - **Pages 5–6 (The Climb):**  
       - Momentum, transformation, scaling the dream.  
       - Combine emotion + data: products launched, adoption, reach.  
       - Page 5 = 3 sentences (momentum starts)  
       - Page 6 = 4 sentences (momentum explodes)  

   - **Page 7 (The Proof):**  
       - The present success — tangible results, metrics, legacy. 
       - Max 12 words per sentence (punchline may use up to 15).
       - Use concrete facts: net worth, company value, reach, employees, impact.  
       - 4 sentences max, one of which lands like a punch.  
       - Example: “He didn’t just win. He rewired the world.”  

   - **Page 8 (The Takeaway):**  
       - Insight, moral, or principle distilled from the journey.  
       - Should be universal — what the reader can apply.  
       - 3 sentences total, reflective and grounded.  
       - Example: “Greatness isn’t built in leaps; it’s carved in quiet persistence.”  

4. **Tone**
   - Direct. Real. No fluff or hero worship.  
   - Every sentence should push the emotional journey forward.  
   - Alternate sentence lengths for rhythm.  
   - When the facts are dry, show what they *felt*.  
   - Think *Shoe Dog* by Phil Knight or *Steve Jobs* by Walter Isaacson.  
   - Keep it cinematic, tense, and human.  

5. **Sentence Rules**
   - 2–4 sentences per page, alternating counts for rhythm.  
   - Never use the same sentence count for more than two consecutive pages.  
   - Limit one long sentence per page (to heighten emotion).  
   - Max 12–15 words per sentence, except for the one ‘long emotional line’ allowed on each page.
   - Keep verbs active; avoid filler or passive phrasing.

6. **Validation**
   - Verify numeric success metrics via reliable sources (Forbes, Bloomberg, SEC filings, etc.).  
   - Verify origin details through reputable interviews or biographies.  
   - If something isn’t verifiable, express the emotional truth, not fiction.  

7. **Image Search Phrases**
   - the person’s name combined with 2–3 key years (beginning, breakthrough, present)  
   - their company or brand combined with 2-3 key years

8. **Final Reminders**
   - Facts anchor; emotion drives.  
   - Avoid lists, timelines, or textbook tone.
   - Every page should *feel alive* — like it’s unfolding now.  
   - The reader should finish feeling moved, not just informed.
